The syllabary — click any character

Read each row across: the columns are the vowel sounds a e i o u v. The last column, v, is not the English letter — it is a nasal vowel, like the u in "but" said through the nose. Two rows hold extra characters in the a column: Ꭷ ka beside Ꭶ ga, and Ꮏ hna and Ꮐ nah beside Ꮎ na. The old character Ᏽ (mv) is no longer used, so it is not on the chart.

One honest note: the syllabary does not mark tone or long vowels, and pairs like ga/ka and da/ta are voicing variants — the first of each pair is softer (between g and k, or between d and t) and the second is stronger, with a puff of air. This chart follows the Oklahoma (Cherokee Nation) conventions.
