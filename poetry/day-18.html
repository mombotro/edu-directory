Day 18 - Image Poems

Image Poems:
Imagist poets, like Ezra Pound and William Carlos Williams, believed a poem could be built from one clear picture, with no explaining. An image is something you can see, hear, smell, taste, or touch. Instead of saying "I felt lonely," the poet shows a picture and lets the reader feel it. Here are two famous examples:

The apparition of these faces in the crowd;
Petals on a wet, black bough.
---Ezra Pound, "In a Station of the Metro" (1913)

so much depends
upon

a red wheel
barrow

glazed with rain
water

beside the white
chickens
---William Carlos Williams, "The Red Wheelbarrow" (1923)

Directions: Choose one ordinary object or scene (a boot by the door, a pot on the stove, a bus window in the rain). Look at it closely, or picture it in your mind. Write an image poem of 3 to 8 lines.
Rules for an image poem:
1. Use concrete words only: things we can see, hear, smell, taste, or touch.
2. Do not explain the feeling. Do not use words like "happy," "sad," or "beautiful."
3. Use at least two different senses.
4. Cut every word that is not doing work.
5. Give it a title that does not give the poem away.
Example (written for this backup, not a student example):

Kitchen, 6 a.m.

steam lifts off the kettle's spout
a spoon ticks once against the cup
the window turns from black to gray
