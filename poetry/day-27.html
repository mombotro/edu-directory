Day 27 - Pantoum

Pantoum:
The pantoum is a poem that comes from Malaysia. It is built from repeating lines, so it feels like a spiral or a slow dance. A pantoum is written in four-line stanzas (quatrains). The pattern is:

Lines 2 and 4 of each stanza become lines 1 and 3 of the next stanza.
In the last stanza, line 2 is line 3 of the first stanza, and line 4 is line 1 of the first stanza.
So the first line of the poem is also the last line.

Here is the pattern, with a letter for each different line:

Stanza 1: A B C D
Stanza 2: B E D F
Stanza 3: E G F H
Stanza 4: G C H A

Directions: Write one pantoum of four stanzas (16 lines). Begin by writing your first stanza, and make each line able to stand in two places: it will be read once as the end of one thought and again as the start of another. Lines may change slightly in meaning when they are repeated, because the lines around them change. Minor changes in punctuation are allowed.
Example (written for this backup, not a student example):

The kettle sings before the sun is up
My grandmother is counting out the spoons
Outside the window, frost on every cup
The radio hums old and tender tunes

My grandmother is counting out the spoons
Steam fogs the window like a breath of ghosts
The radio hums old and tender tunes
She sets the table for the ones she misses most

Steam fogs the window like a breath of ghosts
Each chair remembers someone's weight and laugh
She sets the table for the ones she misses most
The bread is warm; the morning does the math

Each chair remembers someone's weight and laugh
Outside the window, frost on every cup
The bread is warm; the morning does the math
The kettle sings before the sun is up
