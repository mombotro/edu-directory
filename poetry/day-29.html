Day 29 - Performance Poetry

Performance Poetry:
A poem on a page is only half of a poem. When a poem is spoken aloud, the poet's voice, pace, pauses, and movement become part of the poem. Performance poetry (also called spoken word or slam poetry) is poetry written to be heard. Today you will choose a poem and perform it.

Directions: Choose one poem from this unit (your own is best). Prepare it for an audience of family or friends. Practice at least three times before you perform. If you can, memorize it, or at least know it so well that you look up from the page.
Performance checklist:
1. Voice. Speak slowly and clearly. Louder is not always better: a whisper can hold a room.
2. Pace. Slow down for important lines. Speed up for lists and excitement.
3. Pauses. Stop at the end of a line or stanza and let the audience take it in. Count one, two in your head.
4. Eyes. Look at your audience at the end of each stanza.
5. Body. Stand still and straight, or use one or two gestures that match the words. Do not fidget.
6. Breath. Breathe in at the commas and the ends of lines, not in the middle of a thought.
7. Start and finish. Say the title, pause, begin. When you finish, pause, say "Thank you," and wait for the applause.
Example (written for this backup, not a student example). A short poem with performance notes in [brackets]:

[Stand still. Look up. Slow.]

Listen.
[pause]
The house is quiet and it is not quiet.
[softer]
The refrigerator hums its one low note,
the clock is clearing its throat,
the pipes are telling the walls a secret.

[faster, bigger]
And I, who have been quiet all day,
I have so much to say!

[stop. hands down. almost a whisper]
But I say it to the dark,
and the dark says: go on.

[pause. look up. say Thank you.]
